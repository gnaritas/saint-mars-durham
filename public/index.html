The room

Built like a library, tuned like a studio

Sapele wood, black walnut and carpeted floors. Sound panels and clouds on the walls and ceiling soak up the noise so the record stays sharp. Design lead Nellie Vail used a library as the reference: cozy, dark, moody, softer light.

Five tables and a row of barstools in the main room, plus a lounge of plush couches and swivel chairs. Room for a conversation at the next table — never over the music.

Vinyl only

No playlists, no shuffle. Every side plays to the run-out groove.

The name

It started as a play kitchen

Seven years ago, owner Daniel Sartain's daughter called her play kitchen Saint Mars. He told her he would make it real one day. He did — a listening room in the basement of Bar Virgile, the downtown Durham restaurant he owns and runs upstairs.

Questions

Before you come down

Is the music really all vinyl?
Yes. The system plays records only — no playlists, no shuffle. Sides run start to finish, dead air included, from the bar's own collection of about 1,000 records.
What kind of music will be playing?
Jazz, old-school hip-hop, rock 'n' roll, indie rock and electronica — whole records, deep cuts included. A 1970s rock night might run Bob Seger on one turntable and Tom Petty on the other.
Do I need a reservation?
No. There is ample seating indoors at the bar. If you need help before you come down, email barvirgiledurham@gmail.com or call (919) 973-3000.
Is there food?
Bites come down from Bar Virgile, the restaurant upstairs, while you stay for the records.
Why is it called Saint Mars?
It is named after a play kitchen. Seven years ago the owner's daughter called her play kitchen Saint Mars, and he told her he would make it real one day.
