The experience

Records, room, ritual

Saint Mars is built around full records rather than shuffled playlists. You come down the stairs, order something good, and let a side run to the end.

A vinyl-only sound system. A room designed like a library. A short list of cocktails done properly. Here is how the pieces fit.

Track 01 · the sound

No shuffle. No skip. No phone glow.

The system plays vinyl exclusively, drawing on the bar's own collection of about 1,000 records — jazz, old-school hip-hop, rock 'n' roll, indie rock and electronica. A 1970s rock night might put Bob Seger on one turntable and Tom Petty on the other.

A 90-minute visit could include two complete records, deep tracks and some dead air. Between sides there is silence, and the room lets it sit.

Track 02 · the room

Sapele, walnut, carpet — and clouds on the ceiling

The space replaces a loud pink-neon era with sapele wood, black walnut and carpeted floors. Sound panels and clouds on the walls and ceiling keep the room quiet enough for the record to be the loudest thing in it.

Co-owner and design lead Nellie Vail rebuilt it with a library as the reference point — cozy, dark and moody, with softer lighting. The main room holds five tables and a row of barstools, plus a lounge with plush couches and swivel chairs.

Track 03 · the list

A Black Manhattan. A martini with something to eat.

The cocktail list trades tiki drinks for the classics done with intent: a Black Manhattan, a martini served with olives, almonds and giardiniera. Bar Virgile, the restaurant one floor up, sends bites down.
